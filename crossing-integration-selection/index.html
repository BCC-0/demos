One crossing, three acts

A mapped crossing is only a point in OpenStreetMap. Two pipeline stages turn that point into a walkable surface between two sidewalks. The street below is illustrative, with made-up numbers.

Act 1

Why a single attempt goes wrong

The point says "there is a crossing here", not which way it goes or between which sidewalks. If we simply take the nearest sidewalks and draw one strip, it can go wrong in several ways.

One guess is fragile. So the integration stage does not guess once.

Act 2 · crossing integration

Build several candidates on purpose

Instead of choosing, the stage lays an axis through the point, searches outward from both ends and keeps the nearest few sidewalks per end. Every unique pair becomes a candidate strip.

The overlap is intentional. This stage only proposes. It never removes or changes the fused surfaces, and never decides which strip is right.

Act 3 · crossing selection

Score the candidates and pick one winner

Selection runs checks on each candidate. Candidates that pass are ranked by score, and lower is better. Candidates that fail only win if nothing else passes.

Hover or tap a row to see its strip in the scene.

score = length + (1 − road share) × 10 + (1 − alignment) × 10 + off-centre × 1 + height penalty
Illustrative weights. Ambiguous height adds 2. Close-margin threshold is 3.
length road share alignment off-centre height
selected A candidate passed and no passing runner-up is close.
close_margin · this example Another passing candidate (chain:1) is only 2.5 worse, less than 3. The winner stays, but a QA warning is raised for review.
failed Nothing passed. The best eligible candidate is kept anyway and flagged.
unpaired No first end sidewalk was found. The crossing stays a plain strip.
Act 1 shows the problem that motivates the design. Act 2 is crossing_integration_stage, which builds candidates. Act 3 is crossing_selection_stage, which checks, scores and picks. All geometry and numbers on this page are illustrative. Candidates longer than the maximum length are never selected, and every incoming crossing not covered by a winner gets a crossing_selection.dropped finding.
